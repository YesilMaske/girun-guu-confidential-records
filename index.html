02 / Incident

A sharp rise in cursed energy was reported near a residential settlement. A Grade 1 sorcerer was sent to investigate and followed the residual energy to a house already on fire.

Inside, the sorcerer found Haruki unconscious near several deceased civilians. A green mask was fixed to the boy’s face. The object was later identified provisionally as a Cursed Energy Booster.

The settlement had suffered extensive damage, and curse activity was reported throughout the area. The number of curses involved and the full casualty count could not be established from the initial investigation.

The boy was removed from the site and taken for emergency treatment.

The object is believed to have been manufactured to increase the wearer’s cursed energy output. Haruki, however, showed no sign of possessing cursed energy of his own before the incident.

When he put the mask on, it produced an abnormal surge of cursed energy. Investigators suspect that the surge drew curses into the settlement. The reason for the object’s reaction is still unknown.

No reliable information has been found regarding the object’s maker, previous owner, or how it came into the boy’s possession.

04 / Medical & Follow-Up Records

Haruki was unconscious when found. After regaining consciousness at the medical facility, he recalled the incident and attempted to remove the mask by force. Staff intervened and restrained him before further injury could occur.

He received psychological treatment for approximately two years.

At fifteen, on the advice of his treating physician, he chose the name Girun-Guu and entered Lemonhead Jujutsu High.

The available records do not establish the long-term effects of the incident.

05 / Outstanding Questions

  • Why did the object react to a person with no confirmed cursed energy?
  • Was the surge a defensive response, a malfunction, or a property not yet understood?
  • Did the surge directly cause the curse outbreak?
  • Who owned the object before the incident, and how did Haruki obtain it?

There is not enough evidence to assign responsibility for the incident to the subject. Further examination of the object and its history is recommended.
